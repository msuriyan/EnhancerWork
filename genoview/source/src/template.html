Load indexed BAMs of your crispants (short or long reads), enter the guide sequence (or the cut position) and press Analyse edits. Each sample is reported on its own: no control is needed. Every read spanning the cut-site window is classified as unmodified or as an indel allele; indel sizes are shown as an empirical distribution standardised to % of reads, so samples sequenced to different depths read on the same scale.

What GenoView is

A local, single-file genome browser built on igv.js (the web version of IGV), with UCSC-style navigation and an analysis panel for indels and structural variants. Files are read straight from your disk by the browser — nothing is uploaded. Use Chrome, Edge or Firefox.

Loading

  • Genome: FASTA (indexed for you if there is no .fai; lines must be equal width), bgzipped FASTA with .fai + .gzi, .2bit, or a chrom.sizes file (no sequence). Plain .fa.gz is decompressed in memory (fine for small genomes).
  • Indexes are matched by name: x.bam ↔ x.bam.bai or x.bai; x.vcf.gz ↔ x.vcf.gz.tbi; .csi, .crai, .idx likewise. Drop data and index files together.
  • Finding your reads: each BAM is scanned in the background (small/targeted BAMs exactly, large ones via the index) to build a read density track that shows at every zoom level, and a clickable list of read regions under the BAM in the track list. If the view has no reads after loading, GenoView jumps to them. Small BAMs also draw reads when zoomed out much further than IGV's usual 30 kb.
  • Chromosome names: chr1 and 1 are matched automatically for analysis.

Track controls

Annotation tracks (GFF/GTF/BED/bigBed/VCF…) have names and strand arrows switches in the track list; the top-bar gene names / strand arrows / track names buttons switch them for all tracks at once. Hidden labels also stay hidden in exported figures.

Each BAM has a sort menu: largest deletion first, smallest deletion first, largest indel first (deletions from CIGARs and from split reads, anywhere in the view) or random (↻ shuffles again), plus IGV's base / strand / start / mapping quality / insert size sorts at the centre of the view. The sort is re-applied whenever you move.

Arranging panels: drag a track by ⠿ in the track list (or use ▲ ▼) to change its position; − / + or the h box change its height, and BAMs have a separate coverage-panel height (cov h). Dragging tracks inside the browser keeps the list in step.

Click a track's name (or ✎) to rename it — the new name is used in the browser, the CRISPR sample lists, figures and exports. Each track in the left panel has a UCSC-style visibility (hide / dense / squish / pack / full), colour and height. Right-click a track in the browser for the full IGV menu (sorting, colouring by strand / insert size / pair orientation, view-as-pairs, autoscale, data range…).

Variant & SV detection

  • Small indels: insertions/deletions in read CIGARs shorter than the SV size, clustered by position and length; allele fraction = supporting reads / depth.
  • Structural variants: split reads (SA tag) give exact breakpoints; discordant pairs (insert > median + k·MAD, wrong orientation, or mate on another chromosome) give approximate ones; long CIGAR deletions/insertions and soft-clip pile-ups add support. Types: DEL, DUP (tandem), INV, INS, TRA (inter-chromosomal).
  • Read-depth CNVs: depth binned across the region, compared with the sample's median (or with the BAM marked control), smoothed and segmented into gains and losses.
  • RNA-seq mode: N operations are splice junctions, not deletions; long inserts from intron-spanning pairs are ignored; depth CNV calling is off.
  • Hiding calls: hide sizes removes calls by length (1-2, <10, >100000); only in keeps calls overlapping your regions of interest; ⊘ on a row hides that call; Mask known variants hides calls matching a BED/VCF of known polymorphisms (small indels: same position ±3 bp; SVs: ≥ 50% reciprocal overlap). Hidden calls are left out of the depth shading and exports; tick show hidden to see them.
  • Merging BAMs: give several BAMs the same group name; their reads are pooled for calling and their depth summed in graphs. The pooled line in the depth graph always sums every analysed BAM.
  • Insert size is estimated from properly-oriented pairs inside the region. Region size is not capped, but whole-chromosome scans of deep human BAMs take minutes.

The caller is designed for exploring and visualising evidence, not as a replacement for a dedicated SV caller (Manta, DELLY, Sniffles…). You can load those callers' VCFs as tracks and compare.

CRISPR edits (crispants, G0 mosaics)

  • Each BAM is analysed and reported on its own: no control or comparison. Enter the guide (20-nt protospacer without PAM; the PAM and strand are found in the current view) or type the cut position as chr:pos (cut after that base). SpCas9/SaCas9 cut 3 bp 5′ of the PAM; Cas12a ~18 nt after a 5′ TTTV PAM.
  • A read counts if its alignment (including split/supplementary parts) spans cut ± window. Indels overlapping the window define its allele; deletions ≥ 50 bp, from CIGAR or from split alignments, are flagged as large. Reads soft-clipped right at the cut are listed as unresolved (often large insertions or vector integration).
  • Histogram: net indel size (insertions − deletions) per read, as % of spanning reads (or % of edited reads), coloured by frame. Bars at the ends collect everything beyond the range. Cumulative shows the empirical CDF.
  • Long reads: detected automatically on the first run (median aligned read length > 1 kb) and the long-read settings are applied: ± 10 bp window, 10 kb flank, homopolymer filter, 1–2 bp indels ignored unless they touch the cut, ± 2 bp allele merging. Percentages are then approximate (± a few %), because sequencing errors near the cut cannot be fully separated from edits.
  • Count: indels or deletions only: deletions only ignores insertions everywhere (edited %, alleles, size distribution, bar plot) and shows the size distribution as % of deletions, adding up to 100%. See How the size distribution is calculated.
  • Several guides: enter all guides (or cut positions) separated by spaces. Each cut is reported separately, plus a combined target that classifies reads across all cuts and labels deletions between cuts as dropouts. Untick a cut to leave it out.
  • Leaving indels out: ignore sizes drops indels by length (e.g. 1-2, >5000); ⊘ on any indel in the allele table hides that exact indel (inherited polymorphism, SNP-linked indel, alignment artefact) and reads carrying only it count as unmodified; Count indels in → regions of interest restricts counting to the regions you type. All of these recalculate instantly without re-reading the BAMs.
  • Indel size spectrum: all indels in all cut windows (or regions) of a sample, counted once per read, binned small → large (default 1–20, 21–50 … >2000 bp; custom cut-points allowed), deletions and insertions separately. With several BAMs ticked you also get a study view: reads pooled, or the mean ± SD of per-sample percentages (each animal weighted equally), plus a study summary table. Edit figure & export lets you change every text, font, size, colour, layout and figure size, and export PNG (150–1200 dpi, DPI stored in the file), SVG, PDF and the data table.
  • Per-sample bar plot (with 2 or more BAMs): one bar per sample for the selected target — % reads carrying a deletion (or % edited, frameshift, in-frame, large deletion, unmodified), each as a percentage of that sample's reads spanning the counting window. Order, colour (or colour by the BAM's group) and value labels can be changed; Edit & export… adds text and saves PNG at any dpi, SVG or PDF.
  • Mosaicism in G0: the number of distinct edited alleles ≥ 5% and ≥ 1% is reported; with ~2 alleles per diploid cell, more alleles imply mosaic editing.

How the size distribution is calculated (CRISPR tab)

Every number is recalculated from the reads in your BAM files — nothing is estimated or smoothed.

  1. Which reads count. A read is its primary alignment plus its supplementary (split) alignments on the same chromosome and strand. Secondary, duplicate and unmapped records and reads below min MAPQ are skipped. A read is counted only if its aligned span covers the whole counting window — cut − W … cut + W (W = the ± window) for every cut, or each region of interest. These are the spanning reads (n).
  2. Which events count. Deletions come from CIGAR D and from the gap between two split alignments of the same read (large deletions in long reads). Insertions come from CIGAR I. An event counts only if it overlaps the window. Each distinct event is counted once per read.
  3. Filters (all optional, all shown in the panel): Count → deletions only drops insertions; events ⊘-hidden as polymorphisms are dropped; ignore sizes drops the sizes you list; the homopolymer filter drops 1–2 bp indels that only lengthen or shorten a run of the same base; min indel off-cut drops events smaller than that size unless they touch the cut. Long-read settings use these to remove sequencing errors.
  4. Size = length of the deletion (bases removed from the reference) or insertion (bases added), in bp.
  5. Binning. Sizes are grouped into the bins chosen in the figure editor (equal width, ranges such as 1–20, 21–50 … >2000 bp, log, or your own cut-points). Keep the last > max bar on, otherwise events above the axis range are not drawn and the bars no longer add up to 100%.
  6. Y value (figure editor):
    • % of deletions (deletions-only mode) = deletions in the bin ÷ all counted deletions × 100. The bars add up to 100%.
    • % of all indel events (indel mode) = events in the bin ÷ all counted deletions + insertions × 100. Deletion and insertion bars together add up to 100%.
    • % of reads = events in the bin ÷ spanning reads × 100 — how common each size is among all reads, edited or not. This does not add up to 100%.
    • count = number of events.
  7. Several samples (the whole dataset). Reads pooled adds up the event counts of all samples and divides by the pooled total, so samples with more reads weigh more. Mean ± SD computes each sample's percentages first and then the mean and SD across samples, so every animal counts equally (samples with no deletions, e.g. uninjected, have no distribution and are left out of the % of deletions mean).
  8. Per-sample bar plot. % reads with a deletion = spanning reads carrying at least one counted deletion (within the ≥ / ≤ size limits you set) ÷ spanning reads × 100.

Worked example (deletions only): 4,000 spanning reads, 2,183 counted deletions, 960 of them 7 bp → the 7 bp bar is 960 / 2,183 = 44.0% of deletions (and 960 / 4,000 = 24.0% of reads).

Checking the numbers. Data TSV in the figure editor exports every bin and the exact count for every length. The script verify_spectrum.py repeats the same calculation independently with pysam: on the test data (short reads and Nanopore long reads, both modes) its counts are identical to GenoView's.

Running in the background

GenoView keeps working while you use other tabs (it avoids the timers Chrome slows down in hidden tabs and holds a lock that stops Chrome freezing the page). Chrome's Memory Saver can still unload a tab you haven't looked at for a long time; GenoView asks before the page is closed or unloaded, and you can exempt it in chrome://settings/performance → Always keep these sites active.

Annotating figures

Export view… (top bar), Annotate… buttons next to the depth and CRISPR charts, and Annotate… in the figure editor open the current figure on a canvas where you can add any number of text boxes (font, size, colour, bold/italic/underline, alignment, background, border, rotation, multi-line), arrows and highlight boxes, drag them into place, add margins for labels outside the figure, and export PNG or JPEG at 96–1200 dpi or any custom dpi, SVG (vector) or PDF. Annotations are kept while the page is open, so re-exporting at another dpi is one click.

Layout & figures

⤢ Full page (right of the analysis tabs) gives each analysis tab the whole window; click again to return to the browser. Layout (top bar) squeezes all annotation tracks — compact or tight rows, panels shrink to their content — and each annotation track has its own rows setting. ideogram / region box hide the chromosome drawing or just its red current-region box. In Export view, Crop trims the figure and Auto-crop cuts it to the drawn content.

Sessions

Session… (top bar) saves a small .genoview.json with everything except the data: genome and track files (by name and size), track order, names, colours, heights, display modes, coverage/reads, sorting, gene-name/arrow switches, the position, Variants & SVs settings/filters/hidden calls/masks, CRISPR guides, settings and hidden indels, figure styles and annotations. To reopen: Session… → Open session file (or drop the .json on the page), then Choose data folder — files are matched by name and size, and the analyses are re-run. Recently saved sessions are listed for one-click reopening. Browsers never give web pages file paths, which is why the folder has to be chosen once per reopen. Session files carry a format version; older sessions open in newer GenoView builds, and newer sessions load what an older build understands.
